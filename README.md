### 🛵 Blinkit Operations Analytics

![Python](https://img.shields.io/badge/Python-3776AB?style=flat-square&logo=python&logoColor=white) ![Pandas](https://img.shields.io/badge/Pandas-130754?style=flat-square&logo=pandas&logoColor=E70488) ![Streamlit](https://img.shields.io/badge/Streamlit-FF4B4B?style=flat-square&logo=streamlit&logoColor=white) ![Plotly](https://img.shields.io/badge/Plotly-3F4F75?style=flat-square&logo=plotly&logoColor=white)

**The story:** I got one CSV: **121,445 rows × 56 columns**. It looked like a single table, but it wasn't. It was **seven separate business tables stacked on top of each other** (orders, delivery, feedback, customers, inventory, campaigns, products), and each row only made sense if you knew which columns were filled in.

So the first job was detective work. I found the split by studying which columns were populated together, un-merged the file into seven clean sub-tables, and checked the math: the row counts added up to exactly 121,445. Orders turned out to be stored as two row types (headers and items) that had to be joined back on `order_id`. All 5,000 matched.

**What the data said:**
- 📦 **5,000 orders**, **₹1,10,09,309** revenue, **₹2,202** average order value
- ⏱️ **69.4% on-time** deliveries, so roughly 3 in 10 orders ran late. Delays track with lower ratings and worse sentiment.
- ⭐ Average rating **3.34 / 5**, with sentiment split almost evenly three ways
- 📣 Marketing ROAS of **1.97x**. Email leads (2.05x), App trails (1.92x).
- 🧴 Household Care and Personal Care lose **51–57%** of inventory to damage, the biggest supply-chain leak in the data

**The honest part:** the data has quirks (order totals don't equal quantity × price, and about 31% of inventory rows show damage above stock received). I flagged them in the dashboard and never silently "fixed" them. Trust in an analysis starts there.

**The dashboard:** seven tabs (Overview, Orders & Revenue, Delivery, Feedback, Marketing, Inventory, Customers) with global sidebar filters, all computed live from the data with no hard-coded numbers.

🔗 [**Live app**](https://blinkitanalysis.streamlit.app/) · [Code](https://github.com/Himanshu-5680/Data-Analysis_Projects/tree/main/Vibe_coding_Projects/Blinkit-Operations-Analytics-Vibe_coding)

### 👥 People Signals: IBM HR Analytics

![Python](https://img.shields.io/badge/Python-3776AB?style=flat-square&logo=python&logoColor=white) ![Streamlit](https://img.shields.io/badge/Streamlit-FF4B4B?style=flat-square&logo=streamlit&logoColor=white) ![scikit-learn](https://img.shields.io/badge/scikit--learn-F7931E?style=flat-square&logo=scikitlearn&logoColor=white)

**The story:** every company loses people, and most dashboards just *count* how many. I wanted one that answers **who is at risk, why, and what HR should do about it**.

I took IBM's HR dataset (**1,470 employees**) and built a five-level decision surface:

1. **KPIs:** what is happening?
2. **Trends:** which direction is it moving?
3. **Drivers:** why? (overtime, role, department, marital status, commute distance)
4. **Risks & Opportunities:** what's next? A risk model, a high-risk employee list, and KMeans segments
5. **Strategic Actions:** what should HR do? Every action is tied to a specific chart.

**Under the hood:** a balanced Logistic Regression baseline, then a GridSearchCV-tuned Random Forest, judged on F1 / ROC-AUC / Precision / Recall, not vanity accuracy. A second model predicts income. Constant columns and ID fields were dropped up front.

**A design choice I care about:** model output is positioned as a *prioritisation aid for human review*, never an automated employment decision. Predicting people's outcomes deserves that caution.

Every level ends with a Key Insights callout, and there's a one-click PDF export of the live dashboard.

🔗 [**Live app**](https://ibmhranalytics.streamlit.app/) · [Code](https://github.com/Himanshu-5680/Data-Analysis_Projects/tree/main/Vibe_coding_Projects/IBM-HR_Analytics)

### 🎬 Netflix Content Strategy & Catalog Analytics

![Python](https://img.shields.io/badge/Python-3776AB?style=flat-square&logo=python&logoColor=white) ![Streamlit](https://img.shields.io/badge/Streamlit-FF4B4B?style=flat-square&logo=streamlit&logoColor=white) ![Plotly](https://img.shields.io/badge/Plotly-3F4F75?style=flat-square&logo=plotly&logoColor=white)

**The story:** what does Netflix's catalog say about its strategy? I used the public `shivamb/netflix-shows` dataset and looked at it the way a content strategist would: what's being added, where's the catalog aging, and where are the gaps?

The app follows the same five-level BI framework (KPIs → Trends → Drivers → Risks & Opportunities → Actions):
- 📈 Year-over-year acquisition trajectory and monthly seasonality
- 🎭 Top genres, movie runtime distribution, and TV season retention
- 🌏 Catalog aging, single-season churn, and **APAC / LATAM whitespace**
- 🧭 A strategic action matrix

**One detail I'm proud of:** the dataset's `rating` field is a maturity label (`TV-MA`, `TV-14`, `PG-13`...), not a quality score. I refused to turn it into a fake IMDb-style rating and grouped it into audience segments instead. Bad assumptions make confident-looking, wrong charts.

The whole platform is one `app.py`, with data-quality checks built in and a Word report (`.docx`) generated on demand from the current filtered data.

🔗 [**Live app**](https://netflixanlaysisproject.streamlit.app) · [Code](https://github.com/Himanshu-5680/Data-Analysis_Projects/tree/main/Vibe_coding_Projects/Netflix)

### 🛒 Supermarket Sales Intelligence Dashboard

![Python](https://img.shields.io/badge/Python-3776AB?style=flat-square&logo=python&logoColor=white) ![Streamlit](https://img.shields.io/badge/Streamlit-FF4B4B?style=flat-square&logo=streamlit&logoColor=white) ![Plotly](https://img.shields.io/badge/Plotly-3F4F75?style=flat-square&logo=plotly&logoColor=white) ![IBM Bob](https://img.shields.io/badge/Built_with-IBM_Bob-052FAD?style=flat-square&logo=ibm&logoColor=white)

**The story:** a multi-branch supermarket chain has sales data but no insight. Managers can't quickly answer *which branch wins, which category pays the bills, and when to plan for peaks*. This project turns **500 transactions** across four branches (Jaipur, Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru) into a six-tab dashboard.

This one is also my purest **Vibe Coding** experiment: I framed the business questions, specified the architecture (a clean split between `analysis.py` for data and `app.py` for UI), and directed **IBM Bob** to build it, including debugging a real `TypeError` and generating a 12-slide PowerPoint report for the business team.

**What the data said:**
- 💰 **₹2,44,411** total revenue across 500 transactions
- 🏆 **Mumbai (Branch C)** is the top branch at ₹72,469
- 🥤 **Beverages** lead categories (22.96% of sales); **Cheese** is the top product
- 📱 **UPI** is the top payment method (27.8%), and over half of revenue is fully digital
- 🎫 **Members** drive most of the revenue, so the loyalty programme matters
- 📅 **April 2026** was the peak month; **Bakery** is the untapped opportunity

The output isn't just charts: it ends with prioritised business recommendations (High / Medium / Low) and a Product × Branch heatmap for deeper digging.

🔗 [**Live app**](https://project-supermarketdashboard.streamlit.app) · [Code](https://github.com/Himanshu-5680/Data-Analysis_Projects/tree/main/Vibe_coding_Projects/Supermarket-Sales-Dashboard-Vibe-Coding)
